Questions

Common questions.

How are you cheaper than an agency?+
No offices, no account layer, and no sales team to fund. We also use modern tooling for the repetitive parts of a build, so the hours that get billed are the ones that actually needed judgement. You are paying for the work rather than the overhead around it.
What do you need from me to start?+
Whatever you already have. Logos, copy, product photos, an old site, a rough sketch, or nothing but a conversation. We work from the state you are in rather than asking you to prepare a brief first.
Can you fix a site someone else built?+
Yes, that is a large part of what we do. Send us the URL and we will tell you honestly whether it is worth repairing or cheaper to rebuild. The initial check costs nothing.
Do I have to take the maintenance plan?+
No. It exists because most clients would rather not think about updates and backups, but the site is yours either way and it will keep running without us.
What counts as a good automation candidate?+
Work that is repetitive, rule based, high volume, and currently done by a person reading and retyping things. Quoting, intake, triage, reporting, data entry between systems. The audit exists to find these rather than guess at them.
Do we have to commit to a full automation rollout?+
No. The audit stands alone and is useful on its own, and the pilot is deliberately small so you can judge the result before committing further. Many clients stop after the pilot, and that is a fine outcome.
Who owns the work at the end?+
You do, in every case. Code, documentation, prompts, domains and accounts are transferred into your name at handover. There is no licence and no dependency on us continuing.
Where are you based and how do we work together?+
We work remotely with clients across time zones, over email and scheduled calls. Most projects need one call at the start and one at handover, and everything between is asynchronous.
